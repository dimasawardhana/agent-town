# 05 — Three archetypes that lean on detail, and may be cut

**What to build:** A Restaurant, a Market and a School join the vocabulary —
each identified by surface detail rather than by outline.

These three are drawn last, and deliberately. A Restaurant is a pitched roof
that must be told from a Cottage by its awning; a Market is a stall row, which
is texture rather than silhouette; a School is a tall pitched roof that must be
told from a Restaurant. All three depend on detail surviving at 91px, which is
the one thing this design cannot assume.

If one of them does not read, **cut it** rather than enlarge it. The vocabulary
stands at nine without any of the three, and the atlas has room for all twelve
— but legibility is worth more than count, and 64 cels of headroom is worth more
than three names.

## Comments

This is the only ticket in the set with an explicitly acceptable outcome of
*shipping fewer things than it set out to ship*. That is the point of sequencing
the detail-dependent archetypes last: cutting one now costs a drawing, and
cutting one after the vocabulary was presented as twelve would cost a
conversation.

If all three survive, the axis reaches twelve and the atlas holds 936 cels of
1408. If one is cut, it holds 904 and the spare grows.

### Measured: two shipped, one cut

**1220 cels, cell 115×101, sheet 2048×8192, ceiling 1296 — 76 cels spare.**

**The Restaurant is cut.** This ticket set out to draw three and allowed any
that did not read to be cut rather than redrawn. The Restaurant lost.

Its identity was meant to be an **awning** across the front — a pitched roof is
already the Cottage's silhouette, so "a pitched roof, a bit lower" would have
passed the distinctness test while the town said nothing. The awning could not
be drawn. Four attempts, all caught by the enclosed-hole invariant:

1. A box projecting forward of the roof. It closes a triangular void between its
   own top face, its side faces and the roof's front wall, and the outline pass
   seals that void as a hole.
2. Scallop beams along its front lip. A thinner pocket, same result.
3. The same surfaces drawn as a footprint and a beam rather than a box, so
   nothing could enclose — and it still failed, because the void is closed by
   the *roof's* front wall, not by the awning.
4. A clerestory box on the ridge instead, straddling the apex, and a flue at the
   apex. Same class again: a gable's apex is a **line**, so anything on it
   either straddles the two slopes or floats above them.

That is the general rule, and it is worth stating plainly: **on a pitched roof,
nothing may be placed at or across the ridge**, because the ridge has no surface
under it. Every other archetype survived it — the Stadium, Hall, Works, Library,
Cottage and Tower all carry furniture without a hole — because their roofs have
areas, not lines.

So the Restaurant is cut rather than shipped as a fifth attempt. It was named in
the original request, and that is the cost: the town says *Market* and *School*
but not *Restaurant*. **The Market and the School both ship**, and both were the
riskier of the three in the way the ticket predicted — the Market is identified
by texture (three stalls, deliberately, since two reads as a hut and four stop
reading as separate things) and the School by a bell breaking its ridge.

The axis is **eleven**, not twelve.
